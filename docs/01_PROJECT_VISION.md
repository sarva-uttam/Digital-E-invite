# 1. Purpose of This Document

This document defines the long-term vision, mission, target market, core customer problems, value proposition, product principles, strategic boundaries, and intended outcomes of the AI Digital Invitation Platform.

It explains:

- why the product should exist;
- who it is intended to serve;
- which customer problems it should solve;
- what should make it meaningfully different;
- how its Mauritian launch context influences the product;
- how the product remains globally available;
- which principles must guide future product decisions;
- what the platform should and should not become.

This document does not finalize pricing, detailed MVP requirements, technical architecture, database structure, technology providers, AI implementation, payment-provider selection, or final user-interface specifications. Those decisions belong in later approved documents.

---

# 2. Working Product Identity

## 2.1 Working Name

The working project name is **AI Digital Invitation Platform**.

This is an approved internal working name, not necessarily the final commercial brand. The final brand name, domain name, logo, visual identity, and legal business identity remain future owner decisions.

## 2.2 Product Category

The platform is a:

> Mobile-first, AI-assisted digital invitation creation, publishing, sharing, and RSVP-management service launching initially in Mauritius and available to customers globally.

It combines:

- invitation design;
- event information;
- culturally appropriate content;
- guest sharing;
- RSVP collection;
- basic guest management;
- hosted invitation pages.

The product is not merely an image generator. Its intended value comes from supporting the invitation process from creation through guest response.

---

# 3. Vision Statement

> To make beautiful, culturally meaningful, and easy-to-manage digital invitations accessible to hosts worldwide, regardless of design skill, technical knowledge, language preference, location, or event budget.

The platform will launch from Mauritius with strong Mauritian localization while remaining globally accessible. Its long-term vision is to become a trusted international platform through which people can create, personalize, publish, share, and manage invitations for important life events.

The product should transform invitations from static images or manually assembled messages into polished digital event experiences.

---

# 4. Mission Statement

The mission is to help individuals, families, couples, event planners, and small organizations create professional digital invitations quickly and confidently.

The platform should:

- reduce the effort required to design an invitation;
- guide customers who do not know where to begin;
- support cultural and linguistic diversity;
- provide strong Mauritian localization at launch;
- make invitations easy to share through familiar channels;
- simplify RSVP collection and guest organization;
- offer clear, understandable packages;
- produce results that feel personal rather than generic;
- remain accessible to non-technical users worldwide.

---

# 5. The Core Problem

Creating and managing an invitation is fragmented.

A host may currently need to:

1. find a designer or template;
2. explain the desired style;
3. write invitation wording;
4. translate or culturally adapt the wording;
5. confirm names, dates, times, and venues;
6. request design corrections;
7. export an image or document;
8. send it manually to guests;
9. track replies across calls and messaging applications;
10. maintain a separate guest list;
11. repeatedly answer questions about the event.

This process can be slow, inconsistent, confusing, or expensive.

Many people know the feeling they want an invitation to create but do not know the design terminology required to express it. General-purpose design tools can be overwhelming, while fixed templates can feel impersonal.

International invitation products may also fail to reflect local languages, currencies, payment preferences, cultural and religious contexts, event traditions, and regional sharing behaviour. In Mauritius specifically, products may not adequately serve multilingual households, multicultural celebrations, Mauritian Kreol, local payment realities, or WhatsApp-centred communication.

---

# 6. Opportunity

The platform can occupy the space between:

- static invitation templates;
- general-purpose design applications;
- freelance graphic designers;
- event-management software;
- expensive custom event websites.

Its opportunity is to provide a guided, localized, end-to-end experience that remains simpler than professional design software and more flexible than a fixed template.

Mauritius is the launch market because it offers a diverse multicultural population, frequent family and religious events, widespread mobile and messaging use, multilingual communication, and customer needs that may be underserved by international platforms.

Mauritius is a launch priority, not a geographic restriction. Customers elsewhere should be able to discover and use the platform, subject to supported payments, languages, laws, service availability, and operational capacity.

---

# 7. Market Scope

## 7.1 Launch Market

The initial commercial launch and primary localization effort will focus on the Republic of Mauritius, explicitly including Mauritius, Rodrigues, Agaléga, St Brandon, and other territories forming part of the Republic where the service is practically accessible.

The launch experience should account for:

- Mauritian rupee pricing where applicable;
- Mauritius time and date conventions;
- local event practices;
- mobile-first usage;
- local payment realities;
- English and French;
- Mauritian Kreol for Mauritian customers;
- multicultural and multi-religious sensitivity.

## 7.2 Global Availability

The product is intended to be globally available from launch rather than restricted to Mauritius.

Global availability does not mean every market will receive identical localization, payment methods, pricing, legal coverage, customer support, or cultural content immediately. Supported-market capabilities must be communicated honestly.

Architecture and product decisions should avoid unnecessary geographic lock-in. Later documents must define:

- supported billing countries and currencies;
- international payment feasibility;
- jurisdictional and data-protection obligations;
- language availability;
- regional content and moderation needs;
- service restrictions where required;
- rollout and support priorities.

Mauritius remains the initial beachhead market and a core part of the product identity.

# 8. Initial Event Focus

Weddings are the explicit primary launch category.

Weddings provide a meaningful starting point because they often involve:

- strong visual expectations;
- cultural or religious traditions;
- large guest lists;
- multilingual families;
- personalized wording;
- RSVP coordination;
- repeated guest communication.

The underlying product should remain extensible to:

- engagements;
- anniversaries;
- birthdays;
- baby showers;
- religious ceremonies;
- graduations;
- corporate events;
- private celebrations;
- community events;
- other invitation-based occasions.

Supporting every event type in the first release is not required.

# 9. Target Users

## 9.1 Primary Users

### Couples and Families

People organizing weddings or important family events who want a beautiful invitation without needing professional design skills.

### Individual Hosts

People organizing birthdays, celebrations, ceremonies, or private events who need a quick and polished invitation.

### Event Planners and Small Event Organizers

Independent planners and organizers who create or coordinate invitations, guest information, and responses for clients or events.

Event planners are a primary customer group, not merely a future secondary audience. However, this vision does not automatically require multi-client workspaces, agency accounts, white-label functionality, team permissions, or other professional features in the MVP. Their exact MVP needs must be assessed in the PRD.

### Small Organizations

Community groups, religious organizations, small businesses, or administrative teams that need polished invitations without enterprise event-management complexity.

## 9.2 Potential Secondary Users

Potential later audiences may include:

- decorators;
- photographers;
- venues;
- invitation designers;
- larger corporate teams;
- schools and institutions;
- other invitation-related service providers.

They should not expand the MVP without explicit approval.

## 9.3 Guest Users

Guests are product users even if they do not create accounts.

A guest should be able to:

- open the invitation easily;
- understand essential event information;
- use the invitation comfortably on a mobile device;
- respond without confusion;
- trust that the page is legitimate;
- avoid unnecessary registration;
- receive an experience appropriate to the event.

The host and guest experiences are equally important to the platform’s reputation.

# 10. Core Jobs to Be Done

## 10.1 Creative Job

> Help me turn my event idea, mood, traditions, and preferences into an invitation that looks intentional and professional.

## 10.2 Writing Job

> Help me create invitation wording that is warm, accurate, culturally appropriate, and suitable for my event.

## 10.3 Technical Job

> Help me publish and share a digital invitation without needing to build a website or understand technical tools.

## 10.4 Administrative Job

> Help me collect guest responses and keep basic guest information organized.

## 10.5 Professional Workflow Job

> Help me, as an event planner, produce and manage invitations for an event without relying on disconnected tools.

## 10.6 Confidence Job

> Help me feel confident that my invitation correctly represents the importance and identity of my event.

## 10.7 Convenience Job

> Let me complete the process from my phone without coordinating several separate applications or service providers.

---

# 11. Value Proposition

> A guided service that transforms a customer’s event details, preferences, language, and cultural context into a polished digital invitation that can be published, shared, and used to collect RSVPs.

The intended benefits are:

- faster creation;
- lower creative difficulty;
- professional presentation;
- culturally relevant results;
- multilingual support;
- convenient sharing;
- fewer disconnected tools;
- easier guest-response tracking;
- greater personalization than a basic template;
- less complexity than professional design software;
- suitability for both self-organizing hosts and event planners.

---

# 12. Product Differentiation

The platform should not compete solely by claiming that it uses AI. AI is an implementation capability, not the complete customer value proposition.

Differentiation should come from the combination of:

1. guided invitation creation;
2. culturally aware personalization;
3. multilingual content;
4. strong Mauritian localization combined with global availability;
5. professional hosted invitations;
6. controlled customization;
7. simple guest and RSVP tools;
8. support for hosts and event planners;
9. transparent packages and usage limits;
10. mobile-first usability;
11. an end-to-end workflow.

The product should feel specifically useful rather than like a generic AI generator with superficial localization.

# 13. Product Principles

## 13.1 Guided Before Complex

The platform should guide users through clear choices rather than require expertise in typography, layout systems, prompt engineering, image-generation settings, or web publishing.

## 13.2 Mobile First

The complete primary journey should work effectively on a smartphone, including account creation, event setup, generation, preview, editing, payment, publishing, sharing, guest responses, and host management.

## 13.3 Global by Design, Localized with Care

The core platform should support international access without pretending that one experience fits every market.

Localization must be deliberate, accurate, and market-specific. Mauritius receives initial localization priority, while other markets may be introduced or enhanced progressively.

## 13.4 Cultural Respect

The platform must not stereotype communities, automatically add sacred symbols without direction, combine religious elements carelessly, claim unverified cultural authenticity, or treat a country or community as one uniform style.

Users must retain control over whether cultural or religious elements appear.

## 13.5 Accuracy Over Decoration

Names, dates, times, venues, contact information, and event instructions are more important than decorative creativity. AI-generated content must never silently change factual event information.

## 13.6 User Control

Users should be able to review generated results, correct factual information, understand meaningful limits, adjust or regenerate content where entitled, and approve an invitation before publication.

## 13.7 Simplicity

The platform should avoid becoming a general-purpose design editor. It should provide controlled, purposeful customization without reproducing the complexity of professional design software.

## 13.8 Trust and Transparency

The platform should communicate clearly about package inclusions, generation and editing limits, payment status, invitation availability, data usage, guest information, AI-generated material, and applicable refund or failure handling.

## 13.9 Privacy by Design

Hosts and guests should not be required to provide more personal information than the service genuinely needs. Guest information must not be repurposed for unrelated advertising or AI training without a lawful basis and appropriate disclosure.

## 13.10 Accessible Quality

The product should feel premium through coherence, reliability, polish, and attention to detail—not unnecessary complexity.

## 13.11 Provider Independence

The vision must not depend on a specific AI, payment, hosting, authentication, storage, or infrastructure provider. Provider choices will be evaluated in later architecture documents.

# 14. Intended Customer Journey

At a vision level, the desired journey is:

1. The host or planner begins a new event.
2. The platform asks understandable questions about the event.
3. The user provides essential event information.
4. The platform presents an appropriate package or entitlement.
5. The platform assists in generating invitation concepts.
6. The user previews and adjusts a selected direction.
7. The user confirms the event information.
8. Payment is completed where required.
9. The invitation is published at a shareable location.
10. The user shares it through familiar channels.
11. Guests view the invitation and respond.
12. The host or planner reviews responses and basic activity.

Exact sequencing, planner workflows, payment timing, editing rules, and MVP requirements will be defined later.

# 15. Language and Localization Vision

The approved language priority is:

1. **English** — primary platform language;
2. **French** — secondary supported language;
3. **Mauritian Kreol** — localized specifically for Mauritian customers;
4. **Russian** — included in the long-term supported language vision.

The platform may also support bilingual or multilingual invitations and suitable combinations of supported languages.

Localization means more than translating interface labels. It may include invitation wording, tone, date and time presentation, currency, address conventions, cultural terminology, formality levels, guest instructions, and market-specific content.

Mauritian Kreol must not be presented as a generic international language option detached from its Mauritian context.

The platform must not imply that machine-generated translation is automatically perfect. Important content must remain reviewable by the user.

The exact language scope and quality standard for the MVP will be defined later. Inclusion in the long-term vision does not automatically require complete MVP support.

---

# 16. AI’s Intended Role

AI may assist with:

- interpreting user preferences;
- proposing creative directions;
- generating visual material;
- drafting invitation wording;
- adapting tone;
- suggesting multilingual variants;
- helping users refine a selected direction.

AI should not:

- invent event facts;
- publish without user review;
- make legal or payment decisions;
- determine religious identity;
- expose personal information unnecessarily;
- make unsupported claims of cultural correctness;
- remove the user’s ability to verify essential information.

The product should remain operationally understandable when an AI provider is unavailable. Exact fallbacks belong in the AI architecture and PRD.

# 17. Business Vision

The platform is intended to become a sustainable global SaaS business launched from Mauritius.

Its business model should balance:

- customer affordability;
- regional purchasing power;
- perceived value;
- AI-generation costs;
- payment-processing costs;
- hosting and storage costs;
- customer support;
- taxation and cross-border obligations;
- operational sustainability;
- reasonable profit;
- fair usage limits.

Pricing is not finalized here. It will be evaluated in `docs/02_BUSINESS_MODEL.md`, with detailed package rules maintained under `/product`.

---

# 18. MVP Vision

The MVP should prove that customers can complete the essential invitation journey and that the business can deliver it reliably.

At a high level, the MVP should test whether users are willing and able to:

- describe their event and desired style;
- receive useful invitation concepts;
- select and refine a result;
- trust the platform with event information;
- pay for the service;
- publish an invitation;
- share it with guests;
- receive RSVPs;
- manage basic responses.

It should also test the needs of both direct hosts and event planners without automatically adding a complete agency-management system.

The MVP should prioritize one reliable complete journey over many incomplete features. Exact scope belongs in `docs/03_MVP_PRD.md`.

---

# 19. Strategic Non-Goals

Unless approved later, the initial product is not intended to be:

- a general-purpose graphic design platform or unrestricted design editor;
- a replacement for professional event planners;
- a full wedding-planning marketplace;
- a social network;
- a public guest directory;
- a ticketing platform;
- a seating-plan system;
- a comprehensive venue-management system;
- a full customer-relationship-management platform;
- an unlimited AI-generation playground;
- a marketplace for every creative service;
- a native mobile application;
- an enterprise event-management suite;
- a system that autonomously publishes AI output without review.

These boundaries protect product focus.

# 20. Trust, Safety, and Ethical Direction

Potential risks include:

- false or deceptive invitations;
- impersonation;
- inappropriate generated imagery;
- hateful or discriminatory content;
- misuse of religious symbols;
- collection of guest data without proper notice;
- public exposure of private event information;
- malicious links or uploaded material;
- abuse of AI-generation features;
- unauthorized access to host, planner, or guest information;
- cross-border privacy and compliance failures.

Detailed controls belong in later security, AI, legal, and product documents.

The vision-level rule is:

> The platform should make legitimate celebration and event communication easier without making deception, privacy violations, cultural harm, or abusive content easier.

# 21. Success Definition

## 21.1 Host Success

A host can create an invitation without design expertise, obtain a result worth sharing, verify essential information, pay confidently, publish without technical assistance, and track responses more easily than before.

## 21.2 Event Planner Success

A planner can use the platform effectively for an event, deliver a professional result to a client, and manage invitation-related information without unnecessary tool fragmentation.

## 21.3 Guest Success

A guest can open the invitation easily, understand the event details, view it comfortably on a phone, respond with minimal friction, and understand how their information will be used.

## 21.4 Business Success

The business can acquire paying customers, deliver invitations at a sustainable cost, support customers reliably, build trust, generate referrals or repeat use, and learn which markets, event types, and packages create genuine demand.

Exact metrics belong in later documents.

# 22. Assumptions Requiring Validation

The following remain hypotheses:

1. Mauritian and international customers will pay for AI-assisted digital invitations.
2. Weddings are the strongest initial launch category.
3. Customers prefer a guided experience over a blank design editor.
4. Multilingual support creates meaningful customer value.
5. Cultural personalization is a differentiator.
6. Hosted invitation pages are more valuable than image-only invitations.
7. RSVP management increases willingness to pay.
8. WhatsApp will be a major sharing channel in Mauritius and some other markets.
9. Tiered or packaged pricing will be understandable.
10. Customers will accept reasonable generation and revision limits.
11. Supported payment options can serve the intended launch and international flows.
12. A self-service product can deliver acceptable results without constant manual intervention.
13. Event planners will find value without requiring a full agency suite at launch.
14. Global availability can be supported without overextending localization, legal, payment, and support capacity.

These assumptions should be tested through customer interviews, competitor research, prototypes, pricing validation, official provider research, landing-page experiments, controlled MVP usage, and support observations.

Unvalidated assumptions must not be presented as proven facts.

# 23. Long-Term Direction

If validated, the platform may expand into:

- additional event categories;
- richer invitation experiences;
- additional languages and regional localization;
- reusable customer profiles;
- advanced event-planner accounts;
- team and branded business accounts;
- advanced guest-management tools;
- additional currencies and payment methods;
- template or design ecosystems;
- invitation-related services;
- deeper international expansion.

Expansion should follow customer evidence, operational readiness, legal feasibility, and approved roadmap decisions.

The ambition is not to accumulate features. It is to become a trusted global platform for creating and managing meaningful digital invitations.

---

# 24. Decision Hierarchy

When evaluating a product decision, use this order:

1. Does it protect essential event information?
2. Does it respect privacy, culture, religion, and user control?
3. Does it improve the complete invitation journey?
4. Does it serve hosts, guests, or event planners in the approved target market?
5. Is it understandable to a non-technical mobile user?
6. Can it work for a global service while supporting careful local adaptation?
7. Can it be delivered reliably and sustainably?
8. Does evidence justify the complexity?
9. Is it consistent with approved documentation?

An impressive feature that weakens clarity, trust, reliability, or focus should not be prioritized.
